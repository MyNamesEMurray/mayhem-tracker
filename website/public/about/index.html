About MayhemStats

MayhemStats shows community-powered tier lists, win rates, and ideal builds for ARAM Mayhem, the limited-time League of Legends game mode where every player drafts augments at level breakpoints on the Howling Abyss. The numbers here are not scraped or estimated — every game in the database was contributed voluntarily and anonymously by players running the free, open-source MayhemStats Tracker desktop app.

Why this exists

Riot's public API doesn't expose ARAM Mayhem's match data — augment picks in particular are simply absent from the match API. The major stats sites bridge the gap with proxy data: item stats borrowed from regular ARAM and augment stats borrowed from Arena. Those are different modes with different balance, pacing, and augment pools, so none of those numbers actually come from Mayhem games. The one place real Mayhem data exists is the League client itself, which keeps the full post-game breakdown of your own matches.

MayhemStats is the community's answer: crowdsource the real thing. The tracker reads matches from each player's own client, and everyone who opts in pools anonymized games into a shared database. Every number on this site comes from actual ARAM Mayhem games contributed by players — there is no other source, which is also why running the tracker and opting in directly makes the stats better for everyone.

Where the data comes from

MayhemStats Tracker records your matches locally by connecting to your own League client. If you opt in to community stats (it is off by default), the app uploads an anonymous record of each game: the champions, augments, items, and combat stat lines of all ten players, plus the match's queue, patch, duration, and region. Nothing that identifies a player — no summoner names, Riot IDs, or account identifiers — is ever uploaded, and the server's database has no columns to store identity even if it were sent. Games are deduplicated, so two friends contributing the same match count it once.

How Score is calculated

Raw win rates are misleading at small sample sizes — an augment that went 3–0 is not better than one that went 120–80. Score answers a different question: not what the win rate was, but how much of it the games behind it will support. It is the lower bound of the Wilson score interval at 95% confidence, on a 0–100 scale:

Score = the win rate, minus however much the sample size leaves in doubt

Read it as "the true win rate is at least this". A 75% win rate over 4 games scores 30.1, because four games cannot rule out a coin flip. A 60% win rate over 200 games scores 53.1, and the same 60% over 2,000 games scores 57.8 — the identical rate scoring higher purely because more games stand behind it. Nothing arrives at the top of a list and slides down; it climbs as it earns the place.

Win rates shown alongside Score are the raw, unadjusted values; entries with fewer than 20 games are marked with an asterisk and muted colors so small samples are never dressed up as certainty.

How tiers work

Tiers (S+ through D) rank entries by Score within the currently filtered data — the top 5% earn S+, the next 10% S, and so on. Two details worth knowing:

  • Augments are tiered within their rarity. Prismatic augments are intentionally stronger than Silvers, so a global ranking would just restate rarity. An S+ Silver augment means "the best thing you can take when the game offers you Silvers."
  • Tiers are relative. The current data always has an S+ and a D; a tier describes standing against the field, not an absolute power level. Badges render dimmed for entries under 10 games.

Ideal builds

Each champion's page ranks items and augments by the same Score, requiring a minimum sample — 3 games — and a record that is not losing before an entry can lead the build. Nothing fills the gap when too little qualifies: a list that falls short says so, because an item that has never won is not a recommendation however often it was built.

Components are left out of the build lists. Ruby Crystal and Recurve Bow carry win rates because they sat in an inventory at the final whistle, not because they were anyone's plan; the test is whether an item builds into something, which keeps the ones that only look like components, since Manamune and Archangel's Staff transform into Muramana and Seraph's Embrace rather than building into them. The free starting item Poro-Snax is excluded too — everyone gets it, so it carries no information.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add my games? Install MayhemStats Tracker, play Mayhem, and flip on Settings → Community Stats. Your match history uploads once, and new games flow in automatically after each session.

Can anyone see my games or name? No. Contributions carry no identity, and the public site can only read aggregate counts — never individual matches. See the privacy policy for the full details.

Can I remove my data? Yes — Settings → Community Stats → Delete my contributions removes everything your install has shared. Matches nobody else contributed are deleted entirely.

Why do some entries have so few games? The community is growing. Every contributor makes the numbers sharper — that's the whole idea.

Is this affiliated with Riot Games? No. MayhemStats is a fan project under Riot's "Legal Jibber Jabber" policy. Riot Games does not endorse or sponsor it.
