The ARAM Mayhem guide

Mayhem is the ARAM variant where augments do the balancing. That single change breaks most of the intuition you brought from regular ARAM, and it breaks most of the stats you can find about it. This page is about the second problem: how to read augment and champion numbers — ours or anyone's — without talking yourself into a build that a coin flip produced.

What makes Mayhem a different game

Every player on the Howling Abyss drafts augments as the game goes on, chosen from a rotating offer in three rarities: Silver, Gold, and Prismatic. You do not pick freely from the whole pool — you pick the best of what you are shown, at the moment you are shown it, on a champion you did not choose either. Three consequences follow, and all of them shape how the stats should be read:

  • Champion strength is downstream of augments. A champion's win rate is partly a statement about which augments in the pool happen to fit it. Balance patches that touch nothing but augments can move champion win rates several points.
  • Choices are constrained, so "best augment" is the wrong question. The question you actually face is "best augment of these three", which is why rarity-relative rankings matter more than one big list.
  • Games are short and snowbally. Item lines that assume a full-length Summoner's Rift or even a standard ARAM curve routinely never get finished, so the items that show up in winning games are not always the ones a build guide would name.

Why ARAM and Arena numbers don't transfer

Riot's public match API does not expose Mayhem's augment data. That gap is why almost every site showing "Mayhem" stats is really showing something else: item and champion stats borrowed from regular ARAM, and augment stats borrowed from Arena. Both substitutions are wrong in ways you can predict:

  • Arena is 2v2v2v2. Augments that shine in a small skirmish arena with no minions, no towers, and round-based resets behave differently in a five-versus-five lane fight with waves, poke, and a single continuous game state. Sustain, wave clear, and long-range poke are worth very different amounts in the two modes.
  • ARAM item stats assume no augments. In Mayhem, a chunk of every player's power comes from outside the shop. Items that exist to cover a champion's weakness are less necessary when an augment already covered it, and items that scale with a stat an augment multiplied are worth more than their ARAM numbers suggest.
  • The augment pools are not the same. An augment list from Arena will include entries Mayhem never offers and miss ones it does.

MayhemStats exists to close that gap with real games instead of proxies: every number on the site comes from actual Mayhem matches, contributed anonymously by players running the free MayhemStats Tracker. The tradeoff is honest and worth stating plainly — a crowdsourced database is smaller than a scraped one. Which brings us to the part of this guide that actually matters.

Sample size is the whole skill

Most people reading a tier list have never been burned by a number; they have only been burned by a build. The two are closer than they look. A 100% win rate over three picks is not evidence — flipping a fair coin three times gives you all heads one time in eight. A 60% win rate over 300 picks is a fact you can build around.

The useful mental tool is the margin of error: how far a measured win rate can sit from the truth by luck alone. Roughly, at 95% confidence:

GamesWin rate is accurate to aboutWhat that means
5±44 pointsNoise. Nothing is knowable.
20±22 pointsA 60% entry could truly be 38% or 82%.
100±10 pointsBig effects are visible; small ones aren't.
500±4.4 pointsNow a 55% pick is meaningfully above average.
2,000±2.2 pointsFine distinctions become real.

Look at that table next to any tier list on the internet, including this one, and you can see how much of a ranking is signal. This is also why our champion pages print the interval next to the win rate, and why a champion with fewer than 20 games is deliberately kept out of search results instead of being dressed up as a build guide.

What we do about it

Sorting by raw win rate hands the top of every list to whatever went 3–0 last week. So MayhemStats sorts by Score, a win rate pulled toward 50% by an amount that depends on how little data supports it:

Score = 100 × (wins + 10) / (games + 20)

Every entry is blended with a 50% prior worth twenty games. A 75% win rate over 4 games scores 54.2. A 60% win rate over 200 games scores 59.5. The confident number wins, which is the correct outcome and the opposite of what a raw sort would do.

Raw win rates are still shown next to Score, because you should be able to see both — but entries under 20 games are muted, tier badges dim under 10, and champion pages simply omit any item or augment with fewer than three games rather than printing a number that cannot mean anything.

Reading a Mayhem tier list properly

  1. Check the sample before the rank. An S+ badge on 8 games and an S badge on 400 are not comparable claims. The games column is the more informative column.
  2. Read augments within their rarity. Prismatics are meant to be stronger than Silvers, so a global augment ranking mostly re-states rarity and tells you nothing you can act on. An S+ Silver means "take this when the game offers you Silvers" — which is the actual decision in front of you.
  3. Treat tiers as relative. Any dataset has a top 5%, so an S+ exists whether or not anything is genuinely broken. Tiers describe standing against the current field, not absolute power.
  4. Filter to the current patch — then check what you gave up. Mayhem gets tuned between runs, so older patches can describe a different mode. But narrowing to one patch can cut the sample by an order of magnitude. If filtering leaves you with 15 games, the wider range's answer is often the better guess.
  5. Prefer stable, repeated signals. An augment that ranks well on a champion, on similar champions, and across two patches is more trustworthy than one that tops a single list this week.

Turning stats into picks

Numbers are a prior, not a plan. A few habits convert them into better games:

  • Match augments to your damage profile, not to a list. Our champion pages print each champion's per-game damage, damage taken, healing, and gold against the mode average. A champion that already takes far more damage than average gets less from another survivability augment than the raw ranking implies, and more from something that converts that frontline time into damage.
  • Let the offer decide, not the tier list. You are choosing between three specific options. The right question is which of those has the best case on your champion in this game state — a strong-on-paper augment you cannot use is worth nothing.
  • Trust popularity when win rate is thin. If nothing has a real sample, the most-built item on a champion is at least the accumulated opinion of a lot of players, which beats a 100%-over-two-games entry.
  • Re-check after a patch. Because augments carry the balance, Mayhem shifts faster than ARAM does. Yesterday's core item can quietly stop being one.

Where these numbers come from

Every game in the database was contributed by a player running MayhemStats Tracker who turned on community stats — it is off by default. The app reads your own matches from your own League client, strips everything identifying, and uploads the champions, augments, items, and combat lines of the ten players in the game, plus queue, patch, and duration. No summoner names, no Riot IDs, no account identifiers; the database has no columns for them. Duplicate games contributed by two players in the same match are counted once. The full detail is in the methodology page and the privacy policy.

This is also the honest answer to "why are some samples so small": there is no scrape behind this site, only contributors. Every install that opts in tightens every interval on every page — including the champion pages currently held back from search for being too thin to trust.
